\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We set out to measure three small QEC decoder kernels on a commodity FPGA card and report their hardware latency and throughput. What we can report is more than a synthesis-level characterisation: all three kernels, fixed with the one-element AXI output buffer their own interface analysis identified as necessary, were built end to end, placed, routed, and exhaustively verified on a live Alveo U55C. Every single-qubit-per-position pattern the exhaustive-verification requirement calls for -- 19,683 for Shor, 6,561 for Steane across three modes, all 16 for Rep-3 -- agrees with an independently cross-checked software model, with zero mismatches. All three kernels close timing at 300\,MHz with positive slack, and all three synthesise to zero post-route BRAM usage against non-zero HLS estimates, a consistent LUTRAM-inference finding rather than a one-off. The decode logic is small, fast, and now confirmed correct on real silicon; the specific interface required to read a result back without elevated host privileges costs 35 to 70 times the decoder's own pipeline depth at the HLS-estimate stage, and we built and tested that fix rather than merely estimating it.

Alongside the hardware result, this revision produced real Monte Carlo evidence, verified against an independently written reference decoder, that substantially revises this line of work's central logical-error-rate claim for the Shor code: the measured rate under IID-depolarising noise is roughly an order of magnitude higher than previously reported, a gap confirmed well outside statistical uncertainty at \(10^7\) shots. It also produced the first circuit-level, multi-round result associated with this work, for the repetition code, and a corrected scalability analysis showing precisely where LUT-based decoding stops being viable.

What remains open is real and stated plainly rather than implied away: only one kernel has any latency measurement at all, and that measurement is a single opportunistic run rather than the rigorous, tail-resolving, C++-hosted measurement a real-time claim needs; no throughput, batching, or multi-compute-unit result exists; and the reconstructed Steane kernel's hardware verification is a verification of this reconstruction, not of whatever kernel produced a prior submission's Steane figures. What has changed, concretely, from where this campaign started is that the central open question -- does the fix this manuscript's own limitations analysis proposed actually work, for every kernel it applies to -- now has a real, hardware-measured, exhaustively-checked answer, and the answer is yes.
